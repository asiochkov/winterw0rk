# TOP 5 MOTION REFERENCES — WINTERWORK

## 1. Linear — precision and invisible quality

Why:
Linear's current design direction explicitly emphasizes fluidity, reducing visual competition, softening separators and making structure felt rather than seen. Its motion language is strongest in micro-interactions: fast feedback, smooth state changes, contextual panels, command interactions and transitions that preserve spatial continuity.

Borrow for Winterwork:
- 100–180ms micro feedback.
- 180–260ms standard transitions.
- transform + opacity instead of layout-jumping.
- shared-element continuity where an element changes context.
- restrained elevation/scale on hover/press.
- progressive reveal for dense information.
- motion that disappears into the product rather than demanding attention.

Do not borrow:
- Linear's exact typography, colors, iconography, or product metaphors.

Sources:
- https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh
- https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui
- https://60fps.design/apps/linear

## 2. Arc / Arc Search — spatial interaction

Why:
Arc Search demonstrates how gestures and layered surfaces can make an interface feel physical. The pinch-to-reveal interaction is a strong example of using gesture as a meaningful interaction rather than decoration.

Borrow for Winterwork:
- layered surfaces;
- controlled scale/zoom;
- contextual reveal;
- depth through z-order and blur;
- gestures only when the gesture has a clear affordance;
- spatial continuity between source and destination.

Use especially for:
- Arc/90-day journey;
- expanding progress details;
- image/content previews;
- contextual sheets;
- command/search surfaces.

Do not borrow:
- Arc's branding or exact playful transitions.

Sources:
- https://60fps.design/apps/arc-search
- https://www.opendesign.cc/en/sites/arc

## 3. Apple Fitness — physical progress and feedback

Why:
Apple's motion guidance treats motion as a way to convey status, feedback and instruction, while adapting behavior to input methods and accessibility settings. Fitness-style progress motion is useful for making completion feel tangible without requiring gamification.

Borrow for Winterwork:
- progress arcs that physically complete;
- number interpolation;
- completion pulses;
- ring/arc progression;
- subtle haptics where platform support exists;
- motion tied directly to user achievement.

Use especially for:
- daily completion;
- Arc progress;
- workout completion;
- streak/progress milestones;
- focus session completion.

Do not borrow:
- Apple-specific rings, colors, branding or exact screen layouts.

Sources:
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion
- Apple Fitness product/UI references should be treated as inspiration rather than copied assets.

## 4. Strava — data storytelling

Why:
Strava demonstrates how movement data can become a visual story. Its Year in Sport experience used animated paths, maps, statistics and personalized story transitions. Current Strava also supports animated activity maps that replay routes and highlight achievements.

Borrow for Winterwork:
- data appearing through motion;
- route/path drawing;
- chart progression;
- stat counters;
- achievement moments;
- sequential storytelling for reports.

Use especially for:
- Personal Report;
- Progress;
- training history;
- Arc milestones;
- performance summaries.

Do not borrow:
- Strava orange, maps, social feed patterns or branded visual language.

Sources:
- https://60fps.design/shots/strava-year-in-sports-review-story-interaction-sequence
- https://strava.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45451263629709-Activity-Replay
- https://manual.studio/work/strava-year-in-sport-2021

## 5. Headspace — calm rhythm

Why:
Headspace is a strong reference for the opposite side of Winterwork's motion system: not every moment should be energetic. Their animation philosophy deliberately preserves a calm environment and uses motion to maintain continuity.

Borrow for Winterwork:
- breathing rhythm;
- slow ambient movement;
- soft fades;
- gentle scale;
- calm session transitions;
- low-frequency background motion;
- no abrupt visual noise.

Use especially for:
- Focus;
- onboarding;
- recovery;
- reflection/journal;
- quiet states;
- rest timers.

Do not borrow:
- illustrations, characters, colors or brand language.

Source:
- https://s3.amazonaws.com/designco-web-assets/uploads/2019/05/animationhandbook.pdf
- https://60fps.design/apps/headspace
